AIC313/CS378: Introduction to Generative Models

Minhyuk Sung, KAIST, Fall 2026

CreativeGen Challenge

Submission Due: December 5 (Saturday), 23:59 KST
Where to submit: KLMS
Presentation Session 1: December 7 (Monday), 10:30 a.m. - 11:45 a.m. KST
Presentation Session 2: December 9 (Wednesday), 10:30 a.m. - 11:45 a.m. KST

Introduction Slides

What to Do

Explore recent advances in image generative models and use them to create visual content in any form, such as 2D images, panoramas, visual illusions, 3D assets (e.g., via score distillation), mesh textures, or videos rendered from your outputs.

Lecture topics such as latent diffusion, classifier-free guidance, and inference-time scaling are a good starting point, but you are strongly encouraged to go further (see Recommended Readings). Aim for creative and artistic outputs that capture attention at a glance.

You must work with the same team as in the FastGen Challenge.

Important Notes

Please read the following carefully. Any rule violation, or failure to cite the code, models, datasets, or papers you used, will result in a zero score.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, ask in the #question channel on Slack BEFORE using it.

1. Generative Models

The only pretrained generative models you may use are:

Model Allowed Checkpoint
Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL) stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0
Stable Diffusion 2 Manojb/stable-diffusion-2-base (a mirror of the original stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-base weights)

✅ Allowed

  • Modifying and training the allowed models yourself: fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA, DreamBooth) and adding new modules (e.g., a ControlNet), as long as your team trains them
  • Training your own generative models from scratch (e.g., NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting)

❌ NOT allowed

Any other pretrained generative weights, even if they are derived from SDXL or SD2:

  • Other image generators and their variants (e.g., the SDXL refiner, SD 3.x, FLUX, SDXL-Turbo)
  • Downloaded add-on weights (e.g., LoRAs, ControlNets, IP-Adapters)
  • Generative models used as tools (e.g., Marigold for depth, TripoSR for 3D, Real-ESRGAN for upscaling)
  • Commercial generative models and services (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney)

2. Non-Generative Models

Pretrained models that do not generate images, videos, or 3D content may be used for guidance, rewards, or verification, such as CLIP, reward models (ImageReward, PickScore), and perception models (SAM, Depth Anything).

3. LLMs and VLMs

You may use AI tools for writing code (following the course's AI Coding Assistant Tool Policy), LLMs for brainstorming text prompts (disclose this in your write-up), and open-weight VLMs as verifiers that only output scores. LLMs/VLMs must NOT produce any visual or spatial content (e.g., images, layouts, meshes), and commercial LLM/VLM APIs must not be called inside your pipeline.

4. Data and Inputs

  • Training data: Existing data may be used only for fine-tuning, and must be free and properly licensed (no paid data or data generated by other models). List every dataset with a link in your write-up.
  • Inference inputs: Outputs must be generated purely by your pipeline from prompts, seeds, and simple inputs authored by your team (e.g., rough sketches, masks). Existing data, including your own photos, must NOT be used as inputs or references.
  • Meshes: Any mesh must be generated by your pipeline or code, or built from simple primitives (e.g., cubes, spheres) in open-source tools such as Blender. Downloaded 3D assets (e.g., Objaverse) are not allowed.

Include all authored inputs (prompts, sketches, meshes, etc.) in your submission.

5. Outputs and Reproducibility

  • Post-processing: Only basic operations (e.g., cropping, resizing, arranging outputs on your poster) are allowed. Manual painting, retouching, or compositing is not allowed.
  • Sample selection: You may select your best results, but report how many samples you generated, how you selected them, and their seeds.
  • Reproducibility: Your code must reproduce every output in your poster and write-up, with inference on a single 20 GB GPU (the KCLOUD vGPU). Code or data that cannot reproduce your results will be considered as not submitted, resulting in a zero score.

What to Submit

Poster and Write-Up Templates

  1. A poster
    • Please use the poster template in the link above.
    • Name the submission file "Team_{TEAM ID}_poster.pdf" (e.g., Team_10_poster.pdf).
    • Format: PDF
    • File size limit: 10 MB
    • If you do not submit your poster by the deadline, you will NOT be allowed to present at the poster session, resulting in a zero score in peer evaluation.
  2. Source code and data
    • Ensure the reproducibility of your work with your provided code and data.
    • Code or data that cannot reproduce your results will be considered as not submitted, resulting in a zero score.
  3. Write-up
    • It is not required to use the template in the link above, but your write-up must include:
      • Project title
      • Teammates' names and student IDs
      • Brief description of the visual content
      • Discussion of technical aspects
      • Discussion of artistic aspects
      • Reproduction steps using your code and data
    • Name the submission file "Team_{TEAM ID}_writeup.pdf" (e.g., Team_10_writeup.pdf).
    • Format: PDF
    • Length limit: Up to 4 pages in A4 size, excluding references.
    • File size limit: 10 MB
    • Properly cite all the code and resources you have used. Missing references will be considered as misconduct, resulting in a zero score.

Evaluation

Your results will be evaluated based on Technology Score and Creativity Score by the instructor, TAs, and your peer classmates.

  • Technology Score [1–5 range]: This indicates the technical novelty and difficulty in creating the visual content.
  • Creativity Score [1–5 range]: This indicates the originality and artistic value of the visual content.

For both, higher is better, and only integer scores are permitted.

Each individual will assign a Technology Score and Creativity Score to the results of all classmates. The scores given by one person to all results will be normalized. Then, the average of these normalized scores for each criterion will determine your final score.

Presentation Sessions

Both sessions will be held online. Odd-numbered teams present on Monday (December 7) and even-numbered teams on Wednesday (December 9).

  • Attendance is mandatory in both sessions, with your camera and mic on all the time.
  • Each team gives a 1-minute presentation, strictly timed. The material (poster, video, slides, demo, etc.) is up to your team. Test it before the session.
  • Stay on standby during the session you present. All discussions must be in English.

FAQ

Q. Can we use publicly released ControlNet, IP-Adapter, or LoRA weights?
A. No. You may add these modules to SDXL/SD2, but your team must train them.

Q. Can we use SDXL-Turbo or LCM-LoRA to speed up generation?
A. No. You may implement your own acceleration (e.g., based on your FastGen work) on top of the allowed models.

Q. Can we fine-tune on our own photos and then use them as img2img inputs?
A. Fine-tuning on your own photos is allowed. Using them as inference inputs is not.

Q. Can we upscale our outputs?
A. Only with classical interpolation or your own method built on the allowed models (e.g., tiled SDEdit with SDXL). Learned upscalers are not allowed.

Q. Can we use a depth estimator to lift our images into 3D?
A. Yes, if it is not built on a generative model (e.g., Depth Anything, but not Marigold).

Grading

There is no late day. Submit on time.

Late submission: Zero score.
Missing any of the poster, code/data, and write-up: Zero score.
Missing citations of code, models, datasets, or papers: Zero score.
Missing other required items in the write-up: 10% penalty for each.
Wrong format: 10% penalty for each.

Absence (at any time) at the sessions: 20% penalty for each session.
Turning off camera or mic: 20% penalty for each session.
Not staying on standby during the session you present: 20% penalty.
